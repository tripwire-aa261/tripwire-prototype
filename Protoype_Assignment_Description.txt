Part II - Prototype

Create a substantial set of technical artifacts that demonstrate your capabilities and provide investors insight into your design process, tradeoffs, and product capabilities.

The prototype should demonstrate a capability directly relevant to your proposed product, customer value proposition, or competitive differentiation. Explain which critical technical or commercial assumptions underlying your business the prototype is intended to validate.

This should be a significant undertaking, on the order of 40-80+ hours of human effort, tied to substantial computing support. This is not meant to solely be for show purposes.

If you 3d print a shape, it should demonstrate something meaningful that may be difficult to glean from a screen alone. If you create a physics based trade-space evaluator, it should allow for real exploration of the design space, including the financial, cost, and revenue implications of varying assumptions. If you are building a prototype of a user interface, it should actually do something useful and interesting. It should not be something that an investor would believe they can recreate themselves in twenty minutes (and there is a lot today they can recreate themselves in twenty minutes).

Describe the remaining technical work required to transition from the prototype to a commercially viable product, including major engineering challenges, testing requirements, manufacturing considerations, and estimated development costs.

Before you begin building, please send the teaching team a one-page summary of what you plan to build and why you believe that is the appropriate choice for your prototype.
