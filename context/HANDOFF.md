## Decisions in force

- **Off-the-shelf radios until Series A** (bladeRF; B210 fallback), our own 8-channel receiver from 2031 Q2. Reason: prove the technology with buyable parts.
- **Price per facility: $25,000 install + $25,000/yr** (also the twin's value-based default since 2026-11-12: `FINANCE.pricing.set_*_usd` in `twin/cost.py`; `ZONE` keeps the data package's old $50,000 + $18,000 for the archived v0.3 viewer), set against the own-receiver cost (Hays floor $19,073 + $3,734/yr at cost + 40 %); larger sites quoted by sensor count. Reason: below every radar system, ~85 % gross margin.
- **Margins 40 % / 40 % define the floor**; fixed subscription, not per alert. **NRE ($400k) is a company cost.**
- **First customers:** Hays State Prison (GA) and Clemson Memorial Stadium (SC; chosen for its towers over Sanford and Atlanta venues). Clemson needs 30 dB, so the pre-seed live demo is at Hays only and Clemson gets recordings. Stadiums are a separate segment in the model (sellable from 40 dB). Stadium pitch: the bowl layer next to an RF detector.
- **Western Europe is the first market abroad, from 2035**, after the company pays its own way, funded from its own cash (decision 2026-11-12; model slider "Sales abroad start"). Airports and bases stay at Series B (hardest requirements, FAA coordination).
- **Export control:** Tripwire may be ITAR (USML XI(a)(3)(xxvii), passive bistatic radar >125 kHz bandwidth below 2 GHz) or EAR ECCN 5A001.g (passive coherent location). Commodity Jurisdiction request moved to 2028 (Seed).
- **Company based in Atlanta** (founders move at Seed). $175k loaded per hire (~$125k average BLS Atlanta wage + ~40 %).
- **No full-time regulatory hire until Series A** (decision 2026-11-12): hourly counsel ~$80k/yr through Seed, Taras coordinates part time, regulatory lead 2029 Q4. Reason: before Series A the legal work is one opinion, two pilot contracts and customers' SAFER SKIES paperwork; saves ~$0.4M.

- **Pre-seed demo:** site visits with one bladeRF sensor (the ESP32 tabletop demo is no longer the pre-seed demo).
- **Do not claim drone-vs-bird classification** until measured at Seed.
- **Stadium sensors alternate between the top of the stands and 6 m poles outside**; prisons use ~6 m poles on the fence; customer provides poles, power and network.

## Open questions

- **Renewals (to fix, noted 2026-11-13):** the model loses 5% of facilities a year evenly, including mid-contract, and assumes every remaining facility renews at the same $25,000 a year forever. Better: keep each facility through its 5-year term, apply a renewal rate (and a possible price change) at the end of each term, and count only signed terms in the backlog (as now). Check the 5% against the pilot partners' answers on what would make them leave.
- **Growth is linear because hiring is fixed (to fix, noted 2026-11-13):** after Series A the model adds a fixed 5 US salespeople a year (plus 1 a year abroad), each winning the same 2 customers a year. So new facilities per year rise by about the same amount every year, and the weighted backlog (5-year contracts, trailing window) grows almost in a straight line ($36-41M a year, 2035-2042). Fix: tie hiring to sales or cash (e.g. salespeople added in proportion to revenue or bookings), add a ramp for new salespeople, and let wins slow as each segment fills up (an S-curve against the facilities that could buy). Then re-run every figure in Draft 5 and the charts.
- **Idea: price by protected area (Sid, 2026-11-13):** quote a price per unit of protected area (e.g. per hectare or per km of perimeter) built from our cost per unit area (sensors needed per area from the twin, times sensor, install and upkeep cost), with a price floor at cost plus the 40% margin so small sites still cover a minimum. This would replace today's flat $25,000 + $25,000/yr per Hays-size facility and the 1x / 1.5x / 4x site-type multipliers, and would let stadiums, airports and large prisons be priced from their actual size. Needs: sensors-per-area from the twin's placement runs at each site, then a pricing model in `twin/cost.py` and the financial model.
- **Private sites are treated inconsistently (noted 2026-11-13; fix after submission):** refineries (128) and nuclear plants (54) are in the model from Series B (`segLater`), but data centers (3,068, Big Idea Memo) are left out because SAFER SKIES gives counter-drone authority to state and local agencies, not private owners, and most refineries and nuclear plants are private too. Plan: put data centers in the Series B group alongside them (detection only, alerts to local police; check nuclear and refinery rules first), add a go-to-market paragraph, then re-run every figure in Draft 5, the prototype tab and the charts. This also raises the market ceiling (about $270M a year today) for the path to $1B. Draft 5 and the model's "Not in the model" box already state the current assumption.
- **Sales-speed basis (teammate comments on Draft 5, 2026-11-13; to fix after submission):** the 9-month sale and 6 facilities per customer have no source. They are uniform guesses for every customer, though a state corrections department buys for dozens of prisons (Georgia 35), a county for one jail and a stadium owner for one venue. Fix: segment customers by type (state DOC, county, stadium, airport) with their own facility count and sales cycle, sourced from the Georgia purchase records and the design-partner interviews.
- Customers of one type are all the same size and willingness to pay (prisons 1x, stadiums 1.5x, airports/bases 4x Hays). Stadiums vary in sensors, and big-event venues would likely pay more (per-event or tiered pricing): to test with design partners. Defense programs and products other than the facility subscription are not modeled (listed in Draft 5 "Sources and assumptions" and the model's "Not in the model" box).
