A drone tripwire made from cell-tower signals

Small receive-only sensors on the prison fence listen for drone echoes of the signals cell towers already send. Nothing is transmitted, so there is no license, no interference and nothing for a drone pilot to detect. This page shows what that would deliver at three real Georgia facilities, and what it would cost.

How it works

1 · ListenEach sensor hears a cell tower twice: once directly, and once bounced off the drone, a moment later and slightly shifted in pitch.
2 · MeasureThe delay gives the drone's distance along an ellipse; the pitch shift (Doppler) separates moving drones from buildings and trees.
3 · LocateSeveral sensors and towers give crossing ellipses: the drone's position, tracked over time and sent to the control room.

What we know, and what we still assume

Shown
  • A published field trial (Fraunhofer, LTE450) detected a 5 kg drone this way; our model reproduces it.
  • Real terrain, buildings and registered towers for all three facilities.
Simulated only
  • Coverage, tracking and accuracy at each facility.
  • Hardware and installation cost with off-the-shelf parts.
Still to prove
  • Enhanced processing gain and interference removal: they decide the remote and city sites.
  • Tower details (direction, power, channel) at each site, and network busyness by hour.
  • Next step: record real LTE signals at two or more sites (the Seed milestone).
