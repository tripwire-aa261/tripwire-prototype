TripwirePassive radar for drone incursions

AA261 · platform prototype v1.1

Hear the drone before it reaches the fence.

Tripwire sensors transmit nothing. They listen for faint drone echoes in cell-tower and digital TV signals that are already in the air, place the drone, and alert the control room while there is still time to act.

The problem

Inmates reach the package before staff do.

Drones drop phones and drugs into prisons, and staff often see them only after the package is on the ground. These are recent cases at the kinds of site we simulate.

How it works

Three steps, no transmitter.

1

Towers already broadcast

Cell towers and digital TV stations light up the sky around every site, day and night. Tripwire uses the strongest ones in the public FCC records.

2

A drone reflects a faint echo

Each sensor hears the direct signal and, far weaker, its echo off the drone, delayed and shifted in frequency by the drone’s motion.

3

Sensors place it and alert

Three or more transmitter-sensor pairs place the drone; two fixes out of three confirm a track and send the alert with a map. One sensor costs – in parts at 1,000 units.

When the track drops

Losing the echo still tells you where it is.

A hovering drone has no Doppler shift, so its echo is cancelled along with the direct signal and the track drops. But Tripwire knows where the drone was, how fast it can fly, and where it would have been seen. A particle filter keeps 1,000 guesses flying from the last fix; every frame with no echo removes the guesses that were moving through covered ground. What is left is where the drone can be.

Many drones at once

A swarm is many echoes, kept apart.

Each drone makes its own echo, at its own delay and Doppler shift, in every transmitter-sensor pair. The tracker decides which echo belongs to which drone. This is the design; the twin does not simulate swarms yet.

a

Separate in range and Doppler

Drones at different distances or speeds fall in different delay-Doppler cells, so each pair sees separate peaks.

b

Match echoes across pairs

Only the right combination of peaks from 3+ pairs meets at one point. Wrong pairings (“ghosts”) do not agree from frame to frame and are dropped.

c

One track per drone

A multi-target tracker (joint probabilistic or multiple-hypothesis association) keeps each drone’s identity through crossings, using speed and heading.

d

Report the group

Count, spread and heading go to the control room, with a search region for any drone that drops out, as for a single drone.

One sensor, three signals, five products

Tripwire sensors only listen. They hear drone echoes in signals already in the air (cell towers and digital TV), find GPS jammers, and point a heat camera at the drone. This prototype simulates the platform at seven real sites, from a prison fence to an Air Force flightline, a border crossing and a park during an event, and costs every product from a parts list.

Seven sites, three kinds of deployment

The fewest sensors that meet the default requirements (90% of the zone covered, a track 80% of the time, 25 m accuracy, the typical drone tracked 300 m before the fence), and how much interference removal that takes: 20 dB is what processing achieves today (ASSUMPTION), 30 and 40 dB are what better processing would add (not yet shown).

Cell towers alone. The cell-tower physics are the same as in the v0.5 twin, and the 5 kg results match it (Hays 4 sensors today, Fulton and Clemson at 30 dB, Hancock at 40 dB). v0.5 led with the 5 kg drone; this page also shows the 0.9 kg drone, which the twin gives a tenth of the echo (0.01 m² against 0.1 m², ASSUMPTIONS). Cell towers alone still find it at Hays today and at Fulton and Clemson with 40 dB of interference removal. The flightline (48 ha, four times Hays) and the port (28 ha) are too large for 24 cell-only sensors with the smaller drone. Digital TV adds stronger transmitters with wider coverage, which is why fewer sensors meet the requirements with it; that TV performance is an estimate until it is measured at Seed.
